A point, a device or a whole connector can carry a `report` table that decides which readings
reach the broker. Pick a preset or a signal, change the policy, and watch what would be
published. The table under the form is what you put on a point, a `[[device]]` or the
`[connector]`.

## How it works

The playground runs the reporting policy of the connector SDK in your browser. Its engine is a
port of the Rust SDK's state machine, and the docs build runs it against every case in the
contract's [report-policy test vectors](/tedge-dot/reference/contract/report-vectors/), the same
vectors the Rust and C SDKs run. So the chart shows what `tedge-dot` does, not an
approximation of it.

A few things to know when reading the chart:

- The signal is **polled** every second. A heartbeat on a polled point is its next poll after
  `max_interval`; a pushed point (OPC UA monitored items, SNMP notifications) is read on demand
  instead.
- A reading held back by `min_interval` is published when the interval ends, **with its own
  timestamp** (the dashed line shows the wait).
- A change in quality (a read that fails, then recovers) is always published at once. The
  playground only draws good readings.

For the full rules, inheritance between connector, device and point, and per-connector notes,
see [Reducing data volume](/tedge-dot/reference/configuration/reducing-data-volume/).
